# First real-author trial

Purpose: discover which interactions help a real author with a long technical document before launching a comparative study. This is not an A/B estimate.

Use one actual draft and its non-sensitive source notes. Select a section where the author has a genuine unresolved choice about argument or module relationships; retain adjacent sections to expose dependencies. Let the author state the reader and intended point in their own words. Do not require an artificially messy document or feed them a sequence of expected corrections.

Use the installed Skill with adaptive working level. Discuss high-impact statements only where a material decision is unresolved; use strict sentence mode only if the author chooses it. Maintain and show the current draft at milestones. The author may accept, correct, question, supply new facts, or switch to full-section drafting whenever useful. Record the interaction and active reading/editing time if practical. At a natural section milestone, ask which explanations helped, which questions were unnecessary, and whether the text expresses their actual position. Do not prolong the trial for a score.

Review any cross-section effects and compare the original/current passages against sources. An independent reader should explain the method, output, and remaining limits from the current text alone. Preserve both useful changes and regressions. Do not reinterpret acceptance as factual validation, or compare this trial to an unrelated old draft as a controlled experiment.

User participation needed: an actual section with supporting material and a short period of natural feedback. An agent may prepare source organization and reader questions, but cannot impersonate the author's preference or time. For comparative effectiveness, follow PLAN.md after this exploratory session.
